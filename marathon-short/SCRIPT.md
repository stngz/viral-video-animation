# The 1904 Olympic Marathon (The Most Chaotic Race in History)

## Research Facts & Sources

* **Event:** The 1904 Summer Olympics Men's Marathon (Games of the III Olympiad).
* **Location & Date:** Francis Field & surrounding hills of St. Louis, Missouri, August 30, 1904.
* **Historical Sources:**
  * Smithsonian Magazine, *"The 1904 Olympic Marathon May Have Been the Strangest Race Ever Run"* (Karen Abbott, 2012).
  * Encyclopædia Britannica & International Olympic Committee (IOC) Official 1904 St. Louis Marathon Records.
  * Charles J. P. Lucas, *The Olympic Games, 1904* (Woodward & Tiernan Printing Co., 1905 — eyewitness official account).
* **The Conditions & Dehydration Experiment:** Chief organizer James E. Sullivan ran the ~24.85-mile (40 km) race in ~90°F+ afternoon heat over unpaved, hilly clay-dirt roads and intentionally restricted water access to study "purposeful dehydration" (only a water tower at 6 miles and a roadside well at ~11–12 miles). Officials' automobiles drove ahead of the runners, kicking up dense clouds of choking dust.
* **Félix "Andarín" Carvajal (Cuba):** A Cuban postman who lost his travel funds gambling in New Orleans and arrived in St. Louis late wearing street shoes, a dress shirt, beret, and long trousers. Fellow Olympian Martin Sheridan used scissors at the starting line to cut his trousers into makeshift shorts. Hungry during the race, Carvajal stopped at an orchard to eat unripe/rotten apples, suffered stomach cramps, took a nap beside the road, and still finished 4th.
* **Len Taunyane (South Africa):** One of the first Black Africans to compete in the modern Olympics; ran barefoot and was chased nearly a mile off course by a pack of feral dogs, still finishing 9th.
* **Fred Lorz (USA):** Suffered cramps around mile 9, hitched an ~11-mile ride in his manager's automobile while waving at spectators and runners, got out after the car broke down near the stadium, and jogged across the finish line first before officials discovered the stunt and disqualified him.
* **Thomas Hicks (USA — Official Winner):** Nearly collapsed from heat and exhaustion; his handlers refused him water and instead gave him doses of strychnine sulfate (a stimulant/rat poison) mixed with raw egg whites and brandy. Hallucinating and unable to run on his own, he was physically carried across the finish line by two handlers while his legs kept shuffling/pumping in mid-air. His winning time was 3:28:53 — the slowest in Olympic marathon history. He collapsed afterward and retired from competitive running.
* **Attrition & Zero Fatalities:** 32 starters, only 14 finishers, 0 deaths. The IOC nearly dropped the marathon from the Olympic program and subsequently instituted regular water stations and strict medical oversight.

## Voiceover Script (249 words, ~64–67 seconds at ~3.7–3.9 words/s)

In 1904, the Olympic marathon was so disastrous that the guy who finished first rode a car—and the real winner ran on rat poison.

St. Louis wanted the grandest race in history across nearly twenty-five miles of dusty hills in ninety-degree heat. To test human dehydration, the organizer put almost no water on the entire course.

Worse, officials drove noisy cars right in front of the pack, blasting thick clouds of red dust into every runner's lungs.

A Cuban mailman showed up in dress trousers, had his pants snipped into shorts at the starting line, ate rotten orchard apples, took a roadside nap—and still placed fourth.

One barefoot runner got chased a mile off course by wild dogs. Meanwhile, American Fred Lorz rode eleven miles in a car, waving at exhausted runners, then jogged across the finish line—until officials caught him.

That left Thomas Hicks in first. When he began hallucinating, his trainers refused him water—and instead fed him raw egg whites, brandy, and strychnine rat poison. Two men literally carried him over the finish line while his legs kept pumping in mid-air.

Luckily, nobody died. Unluckily, over half the runners quit, and his winning time is still the slowest in Olympic history.

Running in ninety-degree heat without water is like flooring a car with an empty radiator—your blood thickens, your body overheats, and your engine seizes.

The committee nearly banned the marathon forever, instead making regular water stations mandatory. As for Thomas Hicks? He took his gold medal, collapsed, and immediately retired from running forever.
