# The 1904 Olympic Marathon — V2 TikTok-Relatable Script (~228 words)

In 1904, the Olympic marathon was so cooked that first place literally took a car—and the real winner ran on rat poison.

St. Louis threw a 25-mile race in 90-degree heat, and the organizer put almost zero water on the track—just to test dehydration.

Worse, officials drove cars right in front of the runners, blasting red dust straight into their lungs.

A Cuban mailman pulled up in dress pants, got them cut into shorts at the start, ate rotten apples, took a nap—and somehow still finished fourth.

Another barefoot runner got chased a mile off the track by wild dogs.

Meanwhile, Fred Lorz rode eleven miles in a car, waved at everyone struggling, then jogged across the finish line—until judges busted him.

That left Thomas Hicks in first. Bro was straight up hallucinating, so his coaches skipped water and fed him raw eggs, brandy, and literal rat poison. Two guys actually carried him over the line while his legs kept running in mid-air.

Somehow nobody died—but half the runners quit, making it the slowest win in Olympic history.

Running in that heat with zero water is like flooring a car with an empty radiator—you overheat until your engine locks up.

After that disaster, the Olympics made water stations mandatory. And Thomas Hicks? He grabbed his gold medal, passed out, and retired on the spot.
