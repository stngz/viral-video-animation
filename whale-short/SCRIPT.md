# The 1970 Exploding Whale of Oregon

## Research Facts & Sources

* **Event:** The 1970 Florence, Oregon Exploding Whale Incident.
* **Location & Date:** Beach near Florence, Oregon, November 12, 1970.
* **Historical Sources:**
  * KATU-TV News Broadcast by Paul Linnman & cameraman Doug Brazil (November 12, 1970).
  * Oregon State Highway Division / Offbeat Oregon History / Newsweek / Mental Floss archival records.
* **The Whale & Engineer:** A 45-foot, 8-ton rotting sperm whale washed up on the beach near Florence. Oregon State Highway Division engineer George Thornton was placed in charge of disposal and packed half a ton (20 cases / 1,000 lbs) of dynamite beneath the whale, hoping to disintegrate it into tiny pieces for seagulls to eat.
* **The Warning & The "Whale of a Deal" Oldsmobile:** Local businessman and WWII military explosives veteran Walter Umenhofer inspected the setup and warned Thornton that half a ton of dynamite was wildly miscalibrated. Thornton brushed off his warning. Umenhofer had just purchased a brand-new Oldsmobile days earlier from a dealership promotion advertising *"Get a Whale of a Deal on a New Oldsmobile."*
* **The Blast & Aftermath:** When detonated, massive 3-foot chunks of blubber rained down over crowds a quarter-mile away. Zero humans were injured, but a huge chunk fell directly onto and flattened Umenhofer's brand-new Oldsmobile (reimbursed by the state). Even the seagulls stayed away, large sections of the whale remained unmoved, and the highway crew ultimately buried the remains with shovels/bulldozers.
* **The Consequence & Twist:** Oregon permanently ended explosive whale disposal. On the 50th anniversary in 2020, Florence residents officially voted to name a nearby public park **Exploding Whale Memorial Park**.

## Voiceover Script — TikTok Style (~211 words, ~70–78 seconds)

In 1970, Oregon engineers tried to delete a dead whale with half a ton of dynamite—and rained blubber on a parking lot.

A 45-foot whale washed up in Florence, Oregon. The smell got so cooked the whole town was gagging, so engineer George Thornton pulled up with zero whale experience—just pure delulu confidence.

He stacked twenty crates of dynamite under it, thinking it’d turn into seagull snacks. An explosives veteran warned him that was way too much. Thornton thought he had crazy aura, shrugged him off, backed everyone up, and slammed the plunger.

Boom. A giant tower of sand and whale shot straight into the sky. Everyone cheered for two seconds—then looked up and started sprinting as huge chunks rained down.

Somehow nobody got hit. Except one massive slab pancaked a brand-new car—owned by the exact veteran who warned them. Bro literally just bought it from a "Whale of a Deal" sale.

Blasting thick blubber on wet sand is like putting a firecracker under cold butter—the sand turns into a cannon and launches chunks everywhere.

Even the seagulls rage-quit, and half the whale didn't even move—so they still had to bury it with shovels.

Oregon banned blowing up whales forever. Fifty years later, the town opened Exploding Whale Memorial Park.
