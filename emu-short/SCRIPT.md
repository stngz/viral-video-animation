# The Great Emu War of 1932
## Research Facts & Sources

* **Event:** The Emu War (Royal Australian Artillery vs. Dromaius novaehollandiae).
* **Location & Date:** Campion district, Western Australia, November 1932.
* **Historical Sources:** 
  * Australian House of Representatives Official Hansard (Debates on Emu Operations, Nov 1932).
  * Major G.P.W. Meredith's Official Field Dispatches, Royal Australian Artillery.
  * Dr. D.L. Serventy, *A Historical Review of the Emu Problem* (Emu, 1953).
* **The Conflict:** Following WWI, soldier-settlers in Western Australia were encouraged to grow wheat. During a drought, 20,000 migrating emus invaded the wheatbelt, demolishing fences and eating entire crops.
* **Military Deployment:** Defense Minister Sir George Pearce dispatched Major G.P.W. Meredith, two gunners, two Lewis automatic machine guns, and 10,000 rounds of .303 ammunition.
* **The Tactics & Failure:** Emus adopted guerrilla-style scattering. In Meredith's own words: *"Each pack seems to have its own leader now... they split into small mobs, making it impossible to bring the guns to bear."* A mounted gun-truck crashed after an emu became entangled in the steering linkage.
* **Official Casualty Count:** Over 9,800 rounds fired; estimated 200–500 emus killed; 0 human casualties. Major Meredith noted: *"If we had a military division with the bullet-carrying capacity of these birds it would face any army in the world."*
* **The Resolution:** Parliament withdrew the army. Bounties and taller vermin-proof fences eventually protected the farms.

## The Script (~65 seconds, 246 words, ~3.8 words/s)

**[0:00 - 0:06 — THE HOOK]**
In 1932, the Australian military officially went to war against twenty thousand birds. And the birds won.

**[0:06 - 0:17 — THE SETTING]**
It was the Great Depression, and Australian wheat farmers were already struggling when twenty thousand migrating emus invaded their fields, eating the crops and trampling fences like six-foot feathered bulldozers.

**[0:17 - 0:31 — THE ACTION]**
Desperate, the government sent in the Royal Australian Artillery, armed with heavy Lewis machine guns and ten thousand rounds of ammunition. Major Meredith planned a simple ambush. But the moment the soldiers opened fire, the emus didn’t panic. They split into small commando squads and scattered in every direction at thirty miles an hour.

**[0:31 - 0:42 — THE SNAP]**
The soldiers mounted a machine gun onto a truck to chase them across the outback. But the dirt tracks were so bumpy the gunner couldn’t aim, and an emu eventually tangled itself into the steering wheel, sending the truck crashing into a fence.

**[0:42 - 0:49 — DEADPAN DOUBLE LINE]**
Luckily, zero human soldiers were killed. Unluckily, it took nearly ten thousand bullets to defeat approximately two hundred birds.

**[0:49 - 0:57 — THE EXPLANATION]**
The military discovered that shooting an emu is like shooting a sprinting feather mattress. Their dense, shaggy coats absorbed bullet impacts, and their massive leg tendons let them out-sprint a military vehicle.

**[0:57 - 1:03 — THE CONSEQUENCE]**
Major Meredith officially ordered a full retreat, conceding total victory to the emus. The Australian Parliament refused to send a second expedition.

**[1:03 - 1:07 — CALLBACK TWIST]**
The government finally solved the crisis years later—not with tanks or machine guns, but by putting up slightly taller fences.
